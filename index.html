Explore Simple, Compound & Complex Sentences

Type 1

Simple Sentence

One independent clause with a subject and a predicate. It expresses one complete thought.

Subject + Verb (+ Object)
  • The dog barked loudly.
  • She runs every morning.
  • Tom and Lisa love pizza.
  • The children played in the park.

Key Features

1 subject 1 verb 1 idea no joining words
Type 2

Compound Sentence

Two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon.

Clause + FANBOYS + Clause
  • I was tired, but I kept going.
  • She studied hard, so she passed.
  • He likes tea; she prefers coffee.
  • We ran fast, yet we missed the bus.

Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

for and nor but or yet so
Type 3

Complex Sentence

One independent clause plus one or more dependent clauses joined by a subordinating conjunction.

Main Clause + Subordinating Conj. + Dependent Clause
  • Because it rained, we stayed inside.
  • She left before the show ended.
  • Although he tried, he couldn't win.
  • When I grow up, I want to travel.

Subordinating Conjunctions

because although when while if since after before unless until
